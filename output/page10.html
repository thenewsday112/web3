James Gunn Says It 'Won't Be Too Long' Before DCU Batman Is Cast, Will Be Close in Age to David Corenswet's Superman

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

James Gunn has revealed that DC Studios is finally getting ready to cast the DCU's Batman.

Speaking on a new podcast about James Gunn's Suicide Squad film, the director and DC Studios boss addressed where he's at when it comes to the next Batman. Although Robert Pattinson is playing the current caped crusader, it's expected that he will exist in his own separate universe, away from James Gunn's DCU, while another Batman will join forces with the likes of Superman.

He noted that it "won't be too long" before they begin casting the next Batman and debunked rumors that he's already been cast. He noted that he's open to casting anyone for the role, even if they've been in a previous DC movie like The Suicide Squad.

"When we do cast the DCU Batman, which isn't very far away, if I gave someone a day role in Suicide Squad that is going to be the best Batman, then I am not going to rule him out from that. I want to get everyone into account. One of the things that excites me about casting is being able to pick a Batman that is actually the best actor for that role. Supermans have always been chosen on who the best actor for that role is. And for some reason, Batmans are always chosen as movie stars."

Gunn also noted that he is taking age into account for the role, and stated that whoever they go with will likely be around the same age as David Corenswet, who plays Superman. He noted that the two characters are pals and contemporaries, which means they can't be too far apart in age. As a result, you can likely expect the next Batman to be in their low to mid-30s, which has historically been the sweet spot for the role, minus Ben Affleck, who was playing an older version of the character.

As of right now, it remains to be seen who will get the job. Batman is a beloved character and one that a lot of actors would love to take a crack at. Gunn also recently noted that Batman is one of the three main characters for DC's future, as one would expect. Some were hoping the role would go to Alan Ritchson, but the actor confirmed he's been having conversations with the studio about a non-Batman role, and DC Studios boss Peter Safran recently revealed they're trying to find a good character for him to play. As Gunn noted though, it could be someone we're not as familiar with. That worked out wonderfully with David Corenswet, who plays a great version of the Man of Steel. If casting is expected to begin soon, we may know who the next Batman is next year.

Peacock has a solid lineup of movies and original TV shows for November, kicking off with the addition of major franchise favorites like Fast & Furious and Spy Kids. First up, there's a six-episode thriller miniseries called The Good Daughter, which adapts the popular 2017 novel of the same name. Then, later this month, we're getting a new comedy series called Dig from the mind of Amy Poehler.

If you're wondering what's worth watching, we've gathered together some of our top picks right here. You can also jump to all of the new releases for November for the full list by day.

What's Worth Watching?

The Fast and Furious: Tokyo Drift - November 1

Technically the third movie in the Fast and Furious franchise (though not chronologically), Tokyo Drift took the series in a totally different direction. This was the first Fast movie directed by Justin Lin and focuses on teenage street racers in Japan. It was the least popular film in the franchise in terms of box office numbers, but it's definitely worth a rewatch.

Read IGN's review.

The Uprising, which was briefly marketed as a Robin Hood origin story, is the latest Andrew Garfield film. With a solid cast of characters, it's worth a watch if you didn't catch it in theaters. As a Peacock exclusive, this is the first time it will be available on a streaming service.

Read IGN's reivew.

All of the Hunger Games movies, including the Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes, are coming to Peacock this month. The Alien franchise has been experiencing a resurgence over the last few years, and that's thanks in part to director Fede Álvarez taking a successful swing with a new standalone movie. Romulus is a blockbuster horror movie that fans of the original movies will absolutely love.

Although we haven't had the chance to watch it yet, Dig has the potential to be a solid comedy series you'll want to check out. Patrick Stewart's Best Roles in Movies and TV

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

2026 has taken a great many beloved celebrities from us. We thought nothing could rival the pain of losing Dolly Parton, but now comes the news that Patrick Stewart has passed away. Stewart, of course, rose to prominence as the lead of Star Trek: The Next Generation in the ‘80s and ‘90s, before going on to headline Fox’s X-Men movies. But even those two venerable franchises are just a taste of Stewart’s brilliant and prolific acting career.

As we come to terms with this profound loss, let’s take a look back at Stewart’s most memorable roles over the years, covering some of cinema’s most iconic heroes and a few dastardly villains for good measure.

Professor Charles Xavier in the X-Men Series (2000-2026)

Casting Patrick Stewart as Professor Charles Xavier had to have been one of the easiest layups in Hollywood history. He’s a famously bald man with a penchant for mentoring up-and-coming heroes with rousing monologues. That’s not to say that Stewart didn’t continuously bring his A-game to the various X-Men movies, especially whenever he shared the screen with Ian McKellen’s Magneto.

We particularly cherish his late-career turn in 2017’s Logan, where he displayed a new level of frailty and regret. And as much as we’ll miss the actor, it’s comforting to know we’ll get one last opportunity to see him play the role in Avengers: Doomsday.

Sejanus in I, Claudius (1977)

The I, Claudius miniseries might have been the most stacked ensemble cast in TV history. It really says something that Stewart stands out so well among that group – Derek Jacobi, Siân Phillips, Brian Blessed, John Hurt! – despite his relatively young age. Yes, it’s more than a little surreal seeing him with a full head of hair, but Stewart truly owns the role of Roman soldier Sejanus.

Darcy Banker in Green Room (2015)

We’re so used to seeing Stewart play morally upright heroes like Captain Picard and Professor X that it’s always something of a shock to see him cast as a villain instead. Stewart was cast very much against type as a Neo-Nazi skinhead ringleader in this 2015 action-thriller from director Jeremy Saulnier, and that’s what makes the role stand out. Stewart is chilling here, bringing a calm but palpable sense of danger to every scene.

Gurney Halleck in Dune (1984)

Before Josh Brolin brought master swordsman Gurney Halleck to life on the big screen, there was Stewart’s version. He may be best remembered for sauntering across the desert battlefield while holding a pug, but Stewart delivered a memorable performance all the same in this campier take on the Frank Herbert novel. You can immediately understand why the warriors of House Atreides would follow their captain into battle without question.

Captain Ahab in Moby Dick (1998)

Stewart showed us a very Ahab-like side of Captain Picard in 1996’s Star Trek: First Contact, so it’s only fitting that he would go on to actually play the obsessive sea captain in a TV movie version of Moby Dick. As you’d expect, Stewart is phenomenal in the role, earning both Golden Globe and Emmy nominations for his performance.

Deputy Director Avery Bullock in American Dad (2005-present)

American Dad is definitely another case of Stewart being cast against type, and once again, that’s precisely why the role works so well. Stewart’s innate charisma and soothing voice clash mightily with the ridiculous situations the series frequently thrusts him into. Bullock is a real highlight of this long-running animated sitcom, and one of the reasons American Dad has come to eclipse Family Guy in popularity.

Scrooge, Tiny Tim and the Rest in A Christmas Carol (circa 1991)

OK, this isn't from a movie or show, but... When not in space or training mutants in Westchester or doing some other crazy sci-fi stuff, Stewart was often on stage. It’s where he came from, of course, as a member of the Royal Shakespeare Company, and as Star Trek: The Next Generation began to wind down, he found himself back doing theater often. One of his most memorable turns on stage was in his one-man-show version of Dickens’ A Christmas Carol, where he played some 40 characters himself! Who could forget the moment where he wept for Tiny Tim? We wept too!

Leodegrance in Excalibur (1981)

Before Star Trek, before X-Men, Stewart was one of King Arthur’s knights! In fact, he was King Arthur’s father-in-law, specifically in John Boorman’s Excalibur. This was a Stewart who was still getting his sea legs in movies, and his role in the film isn’t huge – but it is memorable. “It’s the boy king!” he hollers in that way only Sir Patrick Stewart could, as young Arthur rides to the rescue early in the movie. Looking back on the film now, his presence is powerful.

Captain Jean-Luc Picard in Star Trek (1987-2023)

And then there’s Jean-Luc Picard, a character who we all love today but who wasn’t necessarily welcomed with open arms back when The Next Generation debuted in 1987. Fans are gonna fan, and frankly, first-season Picard did take a bit of getting used to, as even Stewart himself would later admit that he had to acclimate to a life of shooting episodic, American television and being around a kind of wacky cast of co-stars in Jonathan Frakes, Brent Spiner, et al.

But the funny thing about that is, it’s pretty clear that those crazy Americans helped to complete Mr. Royal Shakespeare Company in a way that might never have happened if he hadn’t put on the spacesuit. As Stewart grew into the role of Picard, a humanity emerged that became the bedrock not just of TNG, but really of Star Trek for an entire, well, generation. Make it so, Sir Patrick. Make it so.

What are your favorite Patrick Stewart roles? Let’s discuss in the comments!

Patrick Stewart Dies At 86

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Beloved actor Patrick Stewart has died at the age of 86. The veteran of stage and screen was best known for his many years as Star Trek's Jean-Luc Picard and X-Men's Charles Xavier, though he also had notable roles in video games, including The Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion and Castlevania: Lords of Shadow.

"We announce with grew sorrow the passing of Sir Patrick Stewart yesterday at his home in Los Angeles, in the loving care of his beloved wife Sunny, following a brief and poignant journey with Alzheimer's Disease and Lewy Body Dimentia" a statement on his official Instagram reads. "He has been held in the warm embrace of family and friends, surrounded by live music, prayer, tears, laughter, and the kind of profound emotion that only he could inspire after a lifetime of devotion to us all. We will forever cherish the peaceful ease and reflection on his face, an enduring reminder of his invincible grace."

Stewart's role as Picard made him a household name, and following the conclusion of Star Trek: The Next Generation, he reprised the role in several Star Trek films as well as the series Star Trek: Picard.

Though best known for his live-action roles like Picard and Xavier, Stewart was also known for his iconic voice. Alongside the aforementioned games like Oblivion and Castlevania: Lords of Shadow (and its sequel), Stewart had voice roles in everything from American Dad to The Prince of Egypt. He also narrated the Ted movies, with his friend Ian McKellen taking over the role for the recent Ted series on Peacock.

WoW: Forever Won’t Have Dead Servers

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

World of Warcraft: Forever will avoid repeating WoW Classic's mistakes and will have tools to prevent "dead" or "unsustainable" server rulesets, Blizzard said.

In the past, various WoW Classic servers became "dead" over time, as players either stopped playing or transferred to other servers. Those massive drops in population had major ramifications for players left on the server being unable to find groups for raids or dungeons, as well as for the server's economy and auction house. In some extreme cases, Blizzard gave away free character transfers so players could leave their "dead" servers behind.

While Forever is going a different route with what are essentially mega servers tailored around specific rulesets, some players might still have anxiety when it comes to picking a ruleset that will be a healthy place to play long-term. 

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BVyhnixdPQg

In the latest episode of the WoW: Forever podcast, Blizzard said it has tools it can use if a ruleset's population becomes "unsustainable." Senior game designer Josh Greenfield gave the example of Forever's separate RP and (newly added) RP-PvP server rulesets. He said there were initially concerns about splitting up the RP community, fearing that one or both of the rulesets wouldn't be able to stand the test of time. He said Blizzard now believes "it will be fine," but does have options should the worst happen. 

"If someday down the line, if something happens and one or both of those populations can't quite sustain themselves, we have options if we do need to say link up their auction houses or allow them to group for instanced content," Greenfield said.

Greenfield said it's not something Blizzard will jump to unless absolutely necessary and that it's committed to its current separate-ruleset path. Blizzard debated the idea of rulesets during Forever's development and discussed letting players from different rulesets group together for instanced content or combining auction houses.

However, it landed on wanting to maintain distinct communities, in part because of the differences in server economies when it comes to PvE and PvP rulesets, with Greenfield using an example of how it's easier to farm for gold and items on a PvE server. That in turn opened up debates on whether these cross-ruleset players would be able to trade items with one another and the need for other restrictions.

"Ultimately, these are different mindsets, these are different rulesets," Greenfield said the team decided. "Let's keep them separate for now. That is a part of original WoW that we don't want to just throw out completely. But I think the tech is set up in such a way that if we ever did really feel like we needed to make a change, we could do so."

He said those options are merely "levers we can pull if we need to" and that it's important for Blizzard to "stay flexible." That's good news, and will likely give players worried about choosing the wrong ruleset some relief. 

Blizzard is keeping busy in the handful of days left before Forever's launch. It previously detailed how it's making improvements to Forever's divisive layering system in time for release, and announced major changes to racial abilities. 

In a first for WoW, Forever will be fully playable on a controller. WoW executive producer and vice president Holly Longdale recently said Forever's controller support is a step towards WoW being playable "wherever you want" and "on whatever you want."

WoW: Forever arrives November 4. If you want to be ready for launch day, you can technically make up to three WoW: Forever characters right now.

Cliff Richard is among the numerous famous artists who are lending their music to Rockstar's GTA 6, with Richard's classic tune "Devil Woman" appearing in the Netflix Extended Look. Richard, 86, didn't know about any of that, however, or that GTA 6 is a game and not a TV show.

Speaking to The Times (via Dexerto), Richard said of the partnership with Rockstar for GTA 6: "I was told this happened!" He said his nephew's sons told him about it. "They didn't realize I could sing like that," he said.

Lucia in GTA 6.

Richard was eventually made aware of GTA 6, but the singer doesn't know what that is. “The show is a game show, isn’t it, really? One of the biggest on TV?” he asked.

"Devil Woman" was originally released in 1976, so it's now 50 years old. Richard said he's pretty jazzed about the fact that young people will hear his music.

“People will no longer just say, ‘Oh, Cliff Richard is from the past,’” he said. “Because I am, but that past is what makes everything what it is today."

Titan Quest II is the successor to the beloved original Titan Quest: an action RPG set in a reimagined ancient Greece, where you set out to stop Nemesis, the Goddess of Retribution, from corrupting the Threads of Fate. Since the game entered Early Access on PC in August 2025, we’ve worked hand in hand with our community. With the full release, the story reaches its conclusion, and the complete journey comes to XBOX.

So, what makes Titan Quest II stand out in a genre full of great action RPGs? It starts with the heart of the series: the Mastery system. Instead of locking yourself into a fixed class at the start of your adventure, you pick two Masteries and combine them into a hybrid class of your own. Titan Quest II launches with six Masteries – Earth, Storm, Warfare, Rogue, Forge, and Spirit – and each of them can be freely paired with any other. That’s 15 distinct combinations before you’ve even looked at your gear.

Earth commands fire and stone for devastating area damage. Storm calls down lightning and frost to control the battlefield. Warfare is all about raw physical might, while Rogue strikes from the shadows with poison and piercing blades. Forge, inspired by Hephaistos himself, reinforces your weapons and armor in the middle of a fight and deploys blade traps, sentry turrets, and ferociously fighting automatons. And Spirit lets you summon the dead and draw on your hero’s life force to fuel otherworldly spells. Want to set up blade traps crackling with lightning? Pair Forge with Storm. Rather send the dead ahead while you strike from the shadows? Spirit and Rogue have you covered.

And the customization doesn’t stop at the pairing. Every skill can be shaped with modifiers that do far more than add a few percent of damage – some change how a skill behaves entirely, like turning a sweeping strike into a channeled whirlwind. Attribute points in Might, Agility, Vigor, and Knowledge let you fine-tune your hero even further, and if you ever want to rethink your build from scratch, you can respec at any time (the gods charge a little gold for it, of course).

Did we mention that every hero can use whatever weapons and equipment they prefer? You can wear full plate armor while wielding a frost magic staff, or charge into battle clad in leather while swinging a two-handed axe. And an archer dressed in fine linen works perfectly well with any Mastery. The choice is truly yours.

The second thing that sets Titan Quest II apart is how it plays. Combat is deliberate rather than frantic. Enemies fight in factions and combine their abilities, so reading the battlefield, dodging at the right moment, and smart positioning matter just as much as raw damage. Even your health potions encourage you to stay in the fight: they refill as you deal damage to your enemies. IGN Germany summed up the Early Access version as “the ARPG for everyone who finds Diablo too hectic” – and we’ll happily take that. With Rituals, you also decide for yourself how hard the gods should test you, adjusting the challenge freely to your liking.

Loot matters, too. Items aren’t just bigger numbers; they serve a real purpose for your build, with plenty of equipment options for every class. Socket Charms and Relics into your gear, and use crafting to reroll, add, or upgrade affixes until every piece is exactly what your build needs.

All of this takes place in a beautiful, handcrafted world rooted in Greek myth. Venture across sun-drenched coasts, the Arkadian Plains, the untamed Wild Lands, and even Mount Olympus itself. Discover dark dungeons, uncover hidden groves where legendary monsters lurk, and stumble upon World Quests you can complete simply by finding them. Along the way, you’ll face creatures straight out of legend, from centaurs and gryphons to cyclopes, all set to a soundtrack that blends traditional Greek music with orchestral grandeur. And if you’d rather not take on Nemesis alone, you can explore Greece together with friends in cooperative multiplayer.
