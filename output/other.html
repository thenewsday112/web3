Magic: The Gathering Designers Detail Reality Fracture’s Jace Arc, Echoed Pairs, and Secret Lair Bundles

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Magic: The Gathering’s Reality Fracture set is a Universes Within set after two Universes Beyond sets in succession, but it’s also a little different in that it showcases iconic Magic characters, spells, and more that have been transformed by Jace Beleren.

The Planeswalker has transformed the universe into how he feels it should be, and that means new versions of well-known cards in a sort of “What If?” scenario.

IGN sat down with Yoni Skolnik, former Magic pro and Principal Game Designer, and Meris Mullaley, Worldbuilding Design Manager, to talk about the set’s influences, what comes in a pack, and the Secret Lair bundle.

Magic’s Designers Talk Reality Fracture Influences

Alternate universes are nothing new in pop culture, but I was curious how much last year’s Marvel’s Spider-Man set influenced Reality Fracture. As you can imagine, the genesis of Reality Fracture comes from a much earlier set.

“Some of the ideas go back to Planar Chaos, a set from 2007 that had a lot of What If elements,” Skolnik revealed.

“That set focused on messing with Magic's color pie, putting effects in colors where you wouldn't normally expect them.”

“While we didn't want to return to that exact formula for a What If set, the concept of What If is very compelling as it lets you pay tribute to Magic's devoted fan base.”

“Mark Rosewater [Head Designer] was keen to try the concept again, and it ended up fitting perfectly with the conclusion to Jace's storyline.”

“When developing the Echoed Pairs, most of our learnings came from other Magic IP sets that featured multiple legends,” Mullaley added.

“We chose characters who were (or could plausibly be) active in this current moment of Magic’s timeline. Creatively, we also kept most characters on their home planes rather than showing a multiversal cast of characters arriving on Hexhaven to stop Jace.”

“This helped the story of Reality Fracture keep a narrower focus on the key characters and reduced the amount of “why is this person here” questions.”

“We also see three separate Planeswalker cards for Jace, and we took a similar approach as we did with legends from Edge of Eternities or The Lord of the Rings: Tales from Middle-earth, where we see a character at multiple points along their narrative. Each Jace represents a different point in his journey, reflecting his accumulation of power and readiness to enact his final plan.”

The Jace of the Set

So what about Jace? The blue-hued Planeswalker has been around since Lorwyn in 2007, but has been in the background of events in recent sets.

“In terms of game mechanics, Empower Jace was perfect for making our main character very present even if you only lightly interact with the set,” Skolnik explained.

“For new Magic players who want to go down deeper into the history of Jace, there are a few cards that are specifically stronger with Jace Planeswalker cards, to make the process of going to look back at old cards feel rewarding.”

“Flavorfully, I was very impressed with how the worldbuilding team navigated the character. For new players, we're drawing on timeless fantasy tropes like ‘Dark Wizard's Tower’ and ‘Former Ally Turned Foe’ to make the story approachable, while still having plenty of deeper lore references in the art, and a gripping conclusion to Jace's storyline in the web fiction.”

“It also helps that Jace is such a deeply blue character, which makes understanding his role go hand in hand with understanding blue's place in the color pie. One of the first things many people learn about Magic is that blue players are evil, so having an archetypally blue villain was a natural fit!”

You hear that, blue players? You’re the bad guys here. Still, sticking with multiple versions of cards, I asked about how the Echoed Pairs have been received. For newcomers, Echoed Pairs include normal and Echoverse versions of cards, for example Karn, Argent Defender, and Karn, Gilded Guardian.

“The technology of guaranteeing 1 card from an exciting subset per pack has been very well received since debuting in Dominaria with 1 legend per pack,” Skolnik reveals.

“Going up to 3 legends per pack in Reality Fracture was necessary to guarantee echoed pairs given our printers' constraints, and it made for very exciting packs that fans are loving.”

“I trust we'll one day find another place to deploy this technology of 3 cards in a pack that is linked somehow. This experimentation was not directly related to the 2027 pack changes, but together they show the breadth of problems our production teams face while balancing Magic design's need for constant novelty and reinvention against the reality of what it takes to print all the Magic product for our growing audience!”

Secret Lair Bundle Is Here To Stay

In the time since we spoke with the Wizards of the Coast team, the Reality Fracture Secret Lair Bundle has been delayed. We asked about whether it’ll be sticking around.

“Yes! Secret Lair Bundle will become a staple of Magic Multiverse sets and is expected to appear in those sets going forward.”

“Part of the impetus for launching these bundles was to consolidate Special Bundles into one incredible package that we could repeat set over set. The theming of Reality Fracture and how we explore Jace’s inner sanctum made a lot of thematic sense to use as the jumping-off point for this new product line.”

So, onto the big question then: as someone who can’t believe their luck by packing Craterclaw Colossus and Hexhaven Invigorator, I was curious what the team’s favorite cards from Reality Fracture were.

“I really love both Ajani Planeswalker cards,” Skolnik explained.

“Seeing Ajani Resolute in standard Life Gain decks has been great. A 2-mana Planeswalker is very tricky to balance, but with Planeswalkers such a big part of the set, I really wanted to try and print one here.”

“Ajani Unrelenting has been demolishing in draft, but I’m also excited to see it doing work in Commander decks built around Planeswalkers.”

Mullaley’s pick was spoiler-filled, so look away, lore fans.

“Vindictive Triumph depicts one of my favorite moments in the fiction, where [spoilers] Liliana resurrects her Echoverse doppelgänger as a lich and takes the fight to Jace. I read that moment in an early draft of the story and knew immediately it needed to be a card.”

Crimson Desert: Charting the Unknown Review So Far

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Crimson Desert’s biggest strength is in the sheer volume of stuff it throws at you, allowing you to spend hundreds or even a thousand-plus hours doing everything from hunting animals with a bow to developing a gambling addiction at the tavern. So it’s not very surprising that Crimson Desert’s first paid DLC, called Charting the Unknown, prioritizes adding more on top of its already staggeringly large repertoire. So far, the interesting new activities that mostly focus on oceanic adventures are exactly as compelling as you’d hope, with ship-to-ship combat, underwater exploration, and romance options being among the list of new worthwhile features. On the other hand, existing activities that have added content, like the extra story missions, have mostly been uninspired retreads that don’t do anything to improve upon their established shortcomings. When Crimson Desert came out, I said that its content was a mile long and an inch deep, and for better or worse this DLC seems to say, “Here’s another half-mile.”

The most disappointing part of the DLC so far has been the additional story missions, which are only accessible once you’ve completed the base game’s already dense main story (a feat which only 10% of Steam players have done, judging by the achievements system). At least the handful of missions I’ve completed at this point have mostly served as an expanded epilogue of sorts, with Kliff fighting more difficult versions of old bosses from the existing story, and Damiane and Oongka fleshing out their less substantial stories as well.

I’m not far enough to know if any of these quests eventually become more interesting, but most of their objectives have been an oddly bland retracing of your steps, like going back to spend time with a familiar character and share a moment fishing together or arm wrestle someone to gather information as an apparent callback to an identical moment in the original story. I did get to a new stretch of the map just before getting distracted by the separate seafaring content, so hopefully this part of the DLC will pick up the pace, but so far it’s been about as unimpressive as I expected given the low bar for storytelling in Crimson Desert.

Much like the base content though, most of the effort seems to have been put into things that aren’t related to the story. Although most of my time has been spent dipping my toes into the water-based ship-crafting feature that’s the focus of this expansion, it feels like I’ve only seen the tip of the iceberg, with new resources to obtain, crewmates to hire, and even more activities to learn like harpooning fish and ship-to-ship combat before you can really begin your voyages properly.

It's going to take more time to fully engage with these new activities and judge whether or not they have the sea legs to keep me entertained for longer than a few hours, but at least right now I’m just impressed (yet again) by the fact that these systems exist in this adventure that’s already so damn packed with things to do. Gliding underwater on the back of a sea turtle and captaining your own pirate crew truly just feel completely bizarre in the context of Crimson Desert, and so far that’s been compelling enough to keep me moving forward even if some of the other added content isn’t great.

What I said about Crimson Desert

"There’s lots to love about this quirky, kitchen sink design, and I admire that it’s managed to pack a metric ton of unique mechanics and interesting areas into one package. (March 20, 2026)

Ubisoft has released a fall update on the Assassin’s Creed franchise, providing news big and small on its flagship series. The news includes updates for existing games and smaller but significant insights on the various upcoming Assassin’s Creed games previously announced.

Assassin's Creed Valhalla

In terms of current games, Assassin’s Creed Valhalla is getting a long-requested New Game Plus thanks to a new update coming on November 5. This means you can start a fresh game while bringing over your progression items/level from your previous playthrough. This update will also introduce the following features:

  • An optional progression mode where Gear, Runes, and Masteries all contribute to Eivor's overall Power
  • New runes, achievements, a stash system, and new rewards, including a Master Assassin outfit inspired by Naoe's look in Assassin's Creed Shadows
  • Mithril, a new resource to upgrade Divine Gear and strengthen runes
  • Divine Gear quality for all items

This news comes a few days after New Game Plus arrived in Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced. 

Assassin's Creed Codename Invictis

Codname Invictis, a PvP multiplayer game, remains in development as Ubisoft shares it has incorporated feedback from players who, according to Ubisoft, “saw great potential in the foundations but wanted more of the pillars that define Assassin's Creed.” To this end, Ubisoft has spent months reworking Invictis to reflect this vague feedback and say the game is “now evolving in an exciting direction that better reflects those pillars while staying true to its multiplayer ambitions.”

Assassin's Creed Codename Hexe

As for the next single-player Assassin’s Creed, Codename Hexe, Ubisoft says it will share the next major update on this game in 2027. First announced in 2022, Codename Hexe is set during the 17th-century reign of the Holy Roman Empire, and the era's witch hunts will play a significant role.

Future Assassin's Creed Remakes 

With the release of Assassin’s Creed Black Flag Resynced earlier this year, Ubisoft has given fans what they'll likely see as a disappointing update on potential future remakes: “One thing we want to be transparent about today is that we currently have no other Assassin's Creed remake actively in development. Our teams are already focused on the next chapter of Assassin's Creed titles, including projects you haven't heard about yet, featuring new settings and stories.”

However, Ubisoft adds that this door isn’t closed for good. “Like many of you, we love the idea of revisiting our classic titles, and we'll keep exploring opportunities to bring older titles back,” says Ubisoft.

Assassin's Creed Netflix Series

Ubisoft’s next update concerns the live-action Assassin's Creed series in production for Netflix. They say the show is “nearing completion” in Rome and that award-winning composer Hannah Peel (Insomnia, Rogue Agent, Game of Thrones: The Last Watch) is composing the score. 

Lastly, Ubisoft teases big celebrations are planned for 2027, which marks the 20th anniversary of the Assassin's Creed franchise. It's keeping details to itself for now, but states it's "preparing a year of reveals, updates, rewards, and community moments. "
