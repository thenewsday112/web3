Everything We Know About the DCU's Batman After Clayface

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

James Gunn and Peter Safran’s DCU is growing at a steady clip. In 2026 alone, we’ve seen new movies like Supergirl and Clayface and gotten a deep dive into all things Green Lantern with the Lanterns series. But for many DC fans, there are only two questions that need answering right now. First, is Adria Arjona actually playing Wonder Woman in 2027’s Man of Tomorrow? Second, where the heck is Batman?!?

We can’t help with the first question, but we can tackle the second. The Dark Knight remains elusive despite Clayface taking place in Gotham City. As we explored in our Clayface Ending Explained, don’t expect Batman to put in an appearance in this Batman-adjacent film. And James Gunn recently reiterated that the DCU Batman hasn't been cast yet, despite all rumors to the contrary. So what do we actually know about the DCU version of this iconic hero? Does Batman currently exist in the DCU? Here’s what we know so far.

Update: We've added new details to this article based on Gunn's comments in the recent "Is The Suicide Squad Canon?" podcast special.

Is Robert Pattinson’s Batman in the DCU?

When Gunn and Safran took charge of the DC brand back in 2023, they inherited a few loose ends from the previous regime. That includes Matt Reeves’ Batman franchise, which kicked off with The Batman in 2022 and continued in The Penguin series in 2024. Many fans wondered if Gunn and Safran would simply fold Robert Pattinson’s Batman into their new DCU, making The Batman the unofficial start of this rebooted universe.

However, Gunn has been pretty clear from the start that this isn’t the plan. When he and Safran revealed the Chapter 1: Gods and Monsters slate in 2023, they confirmed their intention to introduce a new version of the Dark Knight in a movie called Batman: The Brave and the Bold. Progress on that film has been slow in the years since, but it’s still very much in the works.

That means DC is in the slightly awkward position of juggling two simultaneous versions of Batman in live-action. Pattinson will reprise his role in the upcoming The Batman - Part II, which is currently slated for release in February 2028. And Gunn and his collaborators will eventually cast a second actor to play a different Batman in The Brave and the Bold (a film which likely won’t hit theaters before 2029 at the earliest). It remains to be seen if the two versions of the characters will continue to coexist or if The Batman - Part II will end up being the swan song for Reeves’ “The Batman Epic Crime Saga.”

We do expect there to be a fairly stark tonal difference separating these two versions of Batman, at least. The Batman and its follow-ups lean heavily into the darker, grittier, more noir-flavored side of the franchise. Gunn’s Batman, we assume, will be a relatively more family-friendly and comics-inspired take on the character, more akin to how 2025’s Superman handled the Man of Steel. Gunn has previously revealed that The Brave and the Bold is heavily inspired by Grant Morrison’s Batman comics and will introduce Bruce Wayne’s long-lost son Damian.

Does Batman Exist in the DCU?

So far, we’ve established that the DCU has its own version of Batman distinct from the one played by Pattinson. The next key question is simple - where is he? Does Batman already exist in the DCU, or is he still an idea lurking in the depths of Bruce Wayne’s psyche?

By now, we have ample evidence to prove that, yes, Batman does exist and is active in the DCU. To date, the only physical appearance of the Caped Crusader has come in Creature Commandos Season 1, where we briefly see a silhouetted Batman looming on a rooftop. That lone appearance doesn’t tell us much about how old Batman is or what his costume looks like, but it’s a start.

Lanterns also includes a handful of nods to Batman. Kelly Macdonald’s character is named Kerry Kane. As a former Gotham native, we have to assume she’s part of the famous Kane dynasty, making her at least a distant cousin to Bruce Wayne. And Batman himself gets a mention in Episode 5 when Hal Jordan (Kyle Chandler) and John Stewart (Aaron Pierre) briefly reconnect in 2026. Hal mocks Earth’s current Green Lantern Guy Gardner when he says, “Ripped off that Bat guy over in Gotham, too. Put a beacon right in the middle of the f***in’ town here so people can summon him.”

And we even have confirmation that Batman exists in the Clayface movie, despite the fact that he never appears or is even directly mentioned. Safran told Collider, “We haven't really talked about what he's doing during the actual events of Clayface. But Batman is there. This is the introduction to the live-action Gotham. We showed a silhouette of Batman in Creature Commandos, so you know he's around, but this is the first step into the world of Gotham for us.”

How Long Has Batman Been Active?

The DCU definitely has a Batman, one who clearly likes to keep a low profile. But how long has he been around? Is he a relatively recent addition to the DCU’s lineup of costumed heroes, or is he more of a grizzled veteran like Hal Jordan?

That’s a hard question to answer based on the evidence we have so far. The DCU seems to unfold more or less in real time, so we can infer that his Creature Commandos cameo happened in 2024. And Clayface also seems to be a fairly contemporary movie. It is worth noting that Batman isn’t mentioned during the 2016 storyline in Lanterns, only the 2026 storyline. So there aren’t really any confirmed sightings of or references to the character before the 2020s.

On the other hand, the fact that The Brave and the Bold is drawing on Morrison’s comic book run suggests the film will be dealing with a somewhat older and more firmly established version of the Dark Knight than we’re seeing in the Reeves-verse. By the time Morrison left his mark on the character, Batman had years of adventures under his belt and a full roster of teen and adult sidekicks backing him up.

Most importantly, Morrison's Batman is old enough to have fathered a preteen son with Talia al Ghul. If their romantic tryst happened while Bruce was already Batman, then he’ll probably be approaching middle age by now. Even if the movie reveals that Bruce hooked up with Talia while he was still traveling the globe and training to become Batman, it stands to reason he’s been Batman for a good while.

The Superman movie revealed that Kal-El first began his superhero career in 2022. We suspect that the DCU Batman has been around at least a little longer than that. Maybe he stayed in the shadows early on, but he’s established enough now that he’s got the Bat-Signal installed atop GCPD headquarters and is whispered about by other superheroes.

Update: Gunn clarified this question on the recent podcast special "Is The Suicide Squad Canon?", revealing that the DCU Batman is roughly the same age as Corenswet's Superman. Superman was established as being 30 during the events of the first movie, so that would probably put Batman in his early to mid-30s by the evtns of The Brave and the Bold. Perhaps a little younger than we would have guessed, which calls into question whether the film is still tackling the Damian storyline.

But does he have a relationship with those other heroes? Is he BFFs with David Corenswet’s Superman yet? Maybe Man of Tomorrow will reveal more in 2027. Until then, we’ll keep patiently waiting for Gunn to announce the Batman casting.

Where Is The Joker?

If we've established that the DCU does indeed have a Batman, one of the first follow-up questions to arise is "Is there also a Joker?" Has Batman faced off with his greatest nemesis yet, or is this a case like the Superman movie, which depicted the first major confrontation between the Man of Steel and Nicholas Hoult's Lex Luthor?

Gunn was pretty evasive on this front when the topic of the Joker came up on "Is The Suicide Squad Canon?". He didn't want to reveal whether the Joker exists in the DCU yet, which naturally raises problems with Harley Quinn's place in the DCU as well.

"Harley’s relationship to the Joker is specific, right?" Gunn said. "Well, we definitely haven’t established the Joker yet in the DCU at all. We’ve established Batman. We’ve seen that he exists. We’ve seen him in Creature Commandos for a glimpse. But we don’t know if Joker exists yet, and, obviously, Joker is a part of Harley’s past."

Ultimately, Gunn's stance in the podcast is that The Suicide Squad is more or less canon to the DCU, with the caveat being that Harley herself is the biggest hurdle. We'll have to see if Joker is featured in The Brave and the Bold and whether the movie treats him as an existing member of Batman's rogues gallery or a new threat plaguing Gotham City.

EA Reportedly Chose An Unannounced Star Wars Game Over BioWare’s Next Mass Effect

EA recently announced it was shutting down BioWare, ending a studio responsible for multiple hit games over 30 years. According to a new report, EA's decision was driven by economics, as it had to choose between funding a new Mass Effect and backing the safer bet, Star Wars: Battlefront 3.

Bloomberg's Jason Schreier reported that EA had to choose between two costly projects, and with the added pressure of paying off $18 billion in leveraged debt after its sale to a business consortium and Saudi Arabia's investment fund, Battlefront 3 made more financial sense. To add insult to injury, Battlefront 3 isn't even in development yet, and it hasn't been officially announced.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dx0b2Xbnnrc

"They chose to invest in Star Wars and shut down BioWare as a result, said the people familiar, speaking anonymously because they were not authorized to talk to the press," Schreier wrote. "Star Wars: Battlefront 3 has not even begun development yet, but EA executives saw it as a less risky, more predictable bet than a new Mass Effect--and the publisher behind Battlefield has always been more comfortable with shooters than RPGs."

Schreier added that "corporate politics" may have also influenced the decision, as one of the studio's key advocates, senior EA exec Laura Miele, was reportedly sidelined when EA Sports veterans David Tinson and Cam Weber were promoted. BioWare also experienced several flops over the last decade, with Mass Effect: Andromeda, Anthem, and Dragon Age: The Veilguard failing to meet sales expectations, which further influenced EA's decision to shut down the studio.

After BioWare's closure, more details on its Mass Effect project have emerged. Yaga Episode 3 Recap and Review

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Warning: This review contains full spoilers for Yaga Season 1, Episode 3. Episodes 1-3 are now streaming on AMC+ in the US and Crave in Canada, with new episodes releasing on Fridays at midnight PT.

Yaga Episode 3 wastes no time diving right back into this eerie, witchy tale, while answering some of our biggest questions after last week’s debut.

We pick up right where Episode 2’s cliffhanger left off – on the beach with Logan’s body – prior to getting confirmation from the police that, yes, it is in fact Logan’s lifeless body (not Henry’s) and that blood was found near a window open in his bedroom. There’s a moment of confusion for the viewer as Rapp sees Logan’s eyes jump open, and we see that they are ice blue – like Henry’s.

By now, we know Rapp has been hallucinating more frequently and this is possibly either from the drugs from the night before (because he honestly looks like he’s still high) or something dark and ominous that’s happening to him.

We see a pattern in relation to “time” in Yaga. This is confirmed when Carson recounts what happened the previous night at Henry’s party where we learn that “a few hours” had passed between seeing Logan disappear into his room upstairs, and Carson finding Rapp on the beach. Originally, it seemed like some time had passed, but nothing substantial.

Time Blips

In Episode 1, there was a time blip between text messages Rapp received seemingly one after another in the bar scene while speaking with Katherine. His text from Carson comes in at 8:11pm, and another text – only seconds later – comes in from Angel at 9:41pm. From what we see as viewers, time has stayed the same in the environment of where he is, but something is clearly controlling time in relation to him. (I noticed this after the publishing of the premiere review, and I blame missing these subtle clues on being only human and not a witch myself.)

As we continue on with more unexplained moments in this story, Rapp tries to put a move on Carson in his hotel room, which she rejects. He then goes to lie down on his bed and falls asleep almost too quickly – Carson comes and kneels down next to the bed and we see the clock turn to 3:33 (not the first time we’ve seen this either), while intense, whispering voices begin to take over as she leans in to almost kiss him. Rapp gently opens his eyes and sees Pam, Henry’s ex-girlfriend, seductively pulling a white feather out of his mouth.

He suddenly jolts out of his actual sleep and awakens some time later in the day. We also notice a small circle tattoo on Carson’s wrist before they head over to forensics, which seems to be placed in the shot intentionally for us to refer back to later…

Going to the mortuary, we see Logan’s corpse – well, most of it. In the episode’s biggest WTF moment, we learn Logan’s penis was severed from his body and he was castrated. It turns out he was also stabbed with an ancient knife made out of Obsidian and human bone – which Katherine later classifies as an Aztec ceremonial midwife sacrificial knife, connected to deities with symbols representing sexual desire and fertility. All signs so far point to Logan being murdered, and Carson and the police on staff are searching for more evidence, especially as this new case potentially relates to Henry.

We see Carson walk up to a small church to meet Logan’s mom, Alice (played by Sarah Strange). The dark, moody shots and heavy music come together to create an emotionally beautiful scene of grief between the two women. They sit on a bench in the cemetery together and more is revealed about Logan that we hadn’t known before. That includes Logan changing his hair and working out constantly – like he was trying to become Henry himself, perhaps.

Alice reveals her hate for the Parks and shares how she heard a rumor that Logan and Henry bought one of Pam’s lame hens from her and joked they were going to sacrifice it. She then explains passionately how she could see everything affecting Logan, saying, “Everyday, I’d see that thing growing in him. That thing getting bigger and thicker and darker and pulling him further and further away from God, and closer and closer to the Devil.” Carson asks about Logan’s dad, but Alice dismisses the questions.

While Carson is with Alice, we see Rapp jogging on the beach. Pam happens to be running in front of him. He tries to catch up to her and, as he does, she all of a sudden splits into three versions of herself, distorted in Rapp’s view. She calls his name and he trips over a log, seeing more visions and a sinister mask staring back at him, as he peers up at Pam who is somehow immediately next to him with a cheeky comment.

Before Carson and Rapp decide to head back over to Henry’s house to check for more leads, Carson inquires if Henry and Logan were possibly in a relationship, giving Rapp unintentional fuel to speculate on Carson’s sexuality as well, which she avoids divulging.

"The Maiden, the Mother, and the Crone."

As the duo begin surveying Logan’s room, you can see the pile of bloodied sheets next to the window, and they begin slowly uncovering more and more clues as they go along. Interestingly, they find drug powder in a canister with the same triangle symbol we saw from the previous episodes – a symbol known as the Triskelion – which has multiple interpretations. In Celtic mythology it is said to represent sacred femininity and the Triple Goddess archetype: the Maiden, the Mother, and the Crone. Baba Yaga in Slavic folklore is known as the “Crone,” so we’ll have to wait and see how that relates (if at all) as the series continues.

Carson and Rapp also notice the chicken foot hanging from Logan’s lampshade, previously in Henry’s room – and we can now pretty safely assume it’s from the chicken he and Henry were rumored to have sacrificed.

Just when they are trying to figure out how Logan disappeared from his room the night before, Rapp discovers the bookshelf is actually a secret door into another room… a small, eerie spot illuminated by red lights, despite the rest of the house having no power. A desk with a tape recorder and an archive of dozens upon dozens of cassette tapes are stacked on shelves.

As they dig in further, Carson and Rapp find out there’s a recording from the previous night in Henry’s bedroom – from when Logan was having sex with the woman wearing the angel wings. We hear Logan demanding to be called “Henry,” making us wonder if Henry was even at the party last night – despite his phone being traced to that location.

Terrible Lizards, available now on XBOX Series X|S and Game Pass, is a first-person platforming game about escaping runaway dinosaur animatronics in an abandoned roadside attraction. That perspective was something we landed on pretty deliberately, through a process of both considering this particular game’s needs, as well as reviewing what we’ve loved about movement in other games.

When I think about the platforming games I’ve always enjoyed most, a lot of it comes down to rhythm. I like being able to move quickly, react to what’s coming, and string things together without constantly stopping to line everything up.

Jumping off the Shoulders of Giants

You can see that reactive flow really clearly in older 2D platformers like Donkey Kong Country or the early Mario games. With a limited screen in front of you, you don’t always have much time to plan. You’re reacting, trusting the mechanics, and finding a rhythm as you move through the level. That’s what I wanted to bring into Terrible Lizards, and the first-person perspective is a big part of bringing that satisfying, rhythmic, reactive gameplay to a 3D platformer.

Games like Mirror’s Edge and Dying Light are the obvious first-person platforming reference points, but we actually wanted Terrible Lizards to feel a little different moment to moment. We were chasing the tighter, more immediate game feel you get from games like Titanfall, Call of Duty, and Overwatch, where simply moving, jumping, and changing direction feels responsive and satisfying. You do see that kind of feel in first-person platforming games like Neon White, and that was closer to the balance we wanted: strong platforming mechanics built on top of controls that already feel good before you even start chaining moves together.

Floaty Empowerment

The controls needed to feel tight and immediate, but we also ended up giving the player a little bit of floatiness in the air. That mostly came from our aerial control system. Once you jump, you have the ability to adjust your trajectory before you land, and that became one of the most important pieces of the movement system.

For someone who isn’t especially confident with first-person platforming, it gives you room to correct yourself. If you jump too far, you can pull back. If you drift left or right, you can make a small adjustment. You have a little extra time to understand where you’re going and make the landing. At the same time, someone who gets really comfortable with the system can exploit that same freedom. They can make harder jumps, move through sections more efficiently, find faster routes, and occasionally bypass parts of a platforming sequence entirely.

That gave us something we were really happy with: a low skill floor, but a high skill ceiling. You don’t have to be amazing at platformers to play the game, but there’s a noticeably different way of moving through it once everything starts clicking. You can absolutely slow down, explore, look for collectibles, and work your way through the environments methodically. But once you understand the movement and start chaining together jumps, slides, wall runs, clambers, ledges, and swings without really thinking about each individual input, there’s a flow state to it that I find really satisfying.

No Fight, All Flight

We also made a very deliberate decision not to give the player weapons or other means to fight back. Your movement is really your main way of dealing with danger. When one of the creatures comes after you, the answer isn’t to turn around and shoot it. You have to move, and that made the monsters a natural way to test everything the player has been learning.

Sometimes that’s a direct chase. Sometimes a creature causes the environment around you to start collapsing and you have to survive. Other times you’re dropped into a more open arena where the monster is loose in the space with you and you have to complete objectives or figure out how to escape. Those moments let us use the same core movement mechanics in different ways without turning every encounter into the same kind of chase.

Between those moments, the game has more traditional platforming, exploration, environmental puzzles, and larger caverns where figuring out how to move through the space is part of the challenge. Then we can tighten things back down and put the player under pressure again. That variety was important to us. We didn’t want the whole game to be six hours of constant sprinting, and we also didn’t want movement to feel like something that only mattered in designated platforming sections.

It’s really the foundation of how you interact with the game. The story, collectibles, exploration, hunt arenas, and New Game Plus content all sit around that, but the movement is what everything keeps coming back to. Once players get comfortable with it, the hope is that they stop thinking about every individual mechanic and just start moving through the world naturally. That’s when the game shines, and obviously when something is chasing you, that familiarity with the movement starts to matter a lot more.

“My Mom Became A Crater” Is A Very Death Stranding Way To Start An Anime

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

The first trailer for the Death Stranding anime spin-off, Death Stranding: Isolations, has dropped, revealing a quick and spooky glimpse at a world that has been ravaged by several extinction events. The brief teaser reveals little, and amusingly, the official synopsis simply reads, "My mom became a crater." But if you are a fan of Hideo Kojima's post-apocalyptic world and setting? There's plenty to glean from this footage through the visuals, and the trailer also reveals the talent behind this project.

https://youtu.be/ruIT6Xez6tw

If you're unfamiliar with the games, Death Stranding takes place in a world where humanity has been brought to the brink of extinction. After Beached Things (BTs) began to roam the land, various cataclysms broke out across the world. BTs would arise via necrosis from deceased humans, and if they made contact with living people, that would result in massive "voidout" explosions that could easily wipe out a city. To make matters worse, BTs are also the root cause of Timefall, a rain that rapidly ages anything it hits, which eventually led the surviving populace to form Knot Cities across the USA, which was subsequently renamed to the United Cities of America.

Death Stranding: Isolations tells an original story within that setting, and it follows a young girl living in a voidout crater--possibly the one caused by her mom, as the trailer suggests. "In the wake of this cataclysm, many were forced to live in absolute isolation--among them, a young girl. Although this lonely existence was forced upon her, she soon grows to prefer solitude, eschewing all opportunities for real human connection and allowing herself to be strung along by fate," the synopsis reads.

The series also has notable talent attached, including director Takayuki Sano (Jujutsu Kaisen). The crew also includes writer Tow Ubukata (Psycho-Pass), character designers Ilya Kuvshinov (Ghost in the Shell: SAC 2045) and Sayaka Koiso (Dorohedoro), and Death Stranding creator Hideo Kojima is listed as an executive producer. E&H Production, the studio behind recent projects like Undead Unluck and the 2026 version of Magic Knight Rayearth, is handling the animation. Death Stranding: Isolations will stream exclusively through Disney+ in 2027.

This is one of several Death Stranding projects in development currently, as Death Stranding: Mosquito from director Hiroshi Miyamoto and ABC Animation Studio was shown off last year, and a live-action Death Stranding movie is also in development. Michael Sarnoski (A Quiet Place: Day One) will be directing the movie, which will tell an original story that aims to "capture the soul" of the games.

Death Stranding isn't the only video game property getting an anime adaptation, as Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice, Bloodborne, and Ghost of Tsushima projects are also in varying stages of development.
