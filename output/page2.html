Going Back To Kingdom Hearts’ Tarzan Level Is About To Make Me Crash Out

Disney and Hulu have officially confirmed what’s coming to both streaming services in November, offering up some reality TV, anime, and festive entertainment for the colder months.

Both streaming services will be light on new movies this month, aside from some festive pick-ups like The Polar Express and Four Christmases. However, Disney+ and Hulu are both getting a ridiculous amount of TV, including ongoing seasons of Dancing with the Stars and Grey’s Anatomy and some new premieres. Disney+ will also be the streaming home for live events like the Country Music Awards.

Why Disney+ and Hulu together? Well, the two services have been slowly merging their libraries since Disney acquired Hulu. The bundle of both services is also pretty much the same price as either standalone subscription.

Highlights Coming to Disney+ and Hulu

Check out some of our top recommendations of what to watch this month, or scroll ahead to the full November streaming lineup by day.

Final Episodes of VisionQuest

VisionQuest premiered back in October, and we'll get our hands on the final few episodes at the start of November. While it was a little slow to start, IGN's Jesse Schedeen is of the opinion that more recent episodes have been a real return to form for Marvel TV.

The series is a direct spin-off from WandaVision, which we also loved, following a rebooted Vision as he seeks to learn more about his existence. What other synthetic being may have some insight? Ultron, of course.

Percy Jackson and the Olympians Season 3

After VisionQuest, the next big Disney+ series in November is a new season of the live-action Percy Jackson and the Olympians. Season 3 will bring the Camp Half-Blood gang to the events of The Titan’s Curse, introducing new cast members to play Artemis, Hera, and Aphrodite.

This notably marks the first time a Percy Jackson adaptation has made it to the third Rick Riordan book, no offense to the live-action movies.

Check out IGN's full review of Season 1

More Pokémon Anime

Disney officially re-acquired the streaming rights to Pokémon earlier this year, and while some of us expected that to lead to a big burst of Pokémon content, Disney’s instead is taking a slower approach.

The service added the first season of the original Pokémon series back in August, and in November, Disney+ will finally get Season 2. Real slow, I know, but at least the streamer has already announced that it will be home to a new stop-motion animated Pokémon series coming next year.

Mickey 17

Between The Drama, Primetime, and Dune Part 3, Robert Pattinson has been having quite the year. If that’s still not enough Pattinson for you, we’d highly recommend checking out Mickey 17, Bong Joon Ho’s follow-up to the award-winning Parasite.

Mickey 17 is a funky sci-fi adaptation of ADSA’s novel by the same name, where Mickey is part of a program of people who are infinitely cloned to complete deadly missions. In his 8/10 review for IGN, Siddhant Adlakha describes it as a "bleakly hilarious sci-fi comedy."

Christmas Classics and New Holiday Specials

We’re gearing up for the holiday season, and while Disney+ is picking up some Christmas classics, it’ll also be the streaming home for plenty of new holiday specials. For example, Mickey’s Home Alone, a Disney-themed play on the Christmas classic, will stream on Disney+ after airing on Disney Jr.

Both services will also get holiday specials for shows that just happen to exist under the Disney and Hulu banner. The Proud Family ‘Wizmas’ will stream on November 14, a Sofia the First holiday episode will drop on November 26, and Family Guy’s holiday special, already revealed as “The Peter Clause,” arrives at the end of the month.

Nintendo Declares November 1 Is Pikmin Day With A Direct Featuring Game News And Animated Shorts

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Nintendo, and especially developer emeritus Shigeru Miyamoto, have been keen to make Pikmin a franchise on par with its other top-tier names like Mario and Zelda. To that end, it has declared November 1 will serve as Pikmin Day, a branding holiday of sorts to serve as a launching point for yearly announcements. 

The debut Pikmin Day Direct surprise-aired today, with Miyamoto walking through a handful of announcements. First, he explained the reasoning behind the date, as the original three Pikmin standing side by side looks sort of like 111. Uh, okay. So we guess it was either November 1 or January 11. That dubious rationale aside, it's a day to celebrate Pikmin, a pet favorite creation of Miyamoto, so we can't really complain. 

Pikmin 4 - Nintendo Switch 2 Edition

The Switch 2 upgrade for Pikmin 4 was already known to be coming on November 12. This part of the presentation was mostly an overview of the new additions, like the microphone voice commands for Oatchi, and a walkthrough of how the Dandori Academy mode works. You'll get to plan out your best route, and then every weekend (Friday, Saturday, and Sunday) you'll get a chance to take an exam to test your strategy. You only get one attempt, however, so it's a bit like a cuter version of Hitman's Elusive Targets. 

A demo of the Switch 2 version is coming on November 2, which will include the opening area and two Dandori Academy stages. 

Pikmin Bloom

A cinematic trailer for Pikmin Bloom announced a new feature, Secret Caves. To find a Secret Cave, you'll need to discover a cave map, which the presentation hinted can usually be found attached to postcards. Plus you can earn special maps by completing exams in the Pikmin 4 Switch 2 Edition. 

Once you've discovered a cave, you can dispatch your Pikmin to explore it themselves. If they encounter a creature a battle will begin, but you can use your whistle to call them back if they're in danger. If they do get defeated, they'll automatically recover after a waiting period. 

If you win the battle in the secret cave, you'll get a new treasure that will be added to a new area of your collection. The update is coming this winter. 

Pikmin Animated Shorts

Finally, Miyamoto introduced Japanese actress Fuka Koshiba, who went viral for her knitted Pikmin hats. After a bit of chitchat about hats and claw machines, Miyamoto and Koshiba introduced a new Hello Pikmin short about a Pikmin hiding from a suspiciously sneaky toad. This was the first of many Pikmin shorts to come, as Miyamoto announced that there will be another coming later in November, followed by monthly Hello Pikmin shorts starting in December.
